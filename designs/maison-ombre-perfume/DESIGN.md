## 1. Overall aesthetic

A slow, bilingual landing page for the invented Parisian house Maison Ombre and its single fragrance, No. 7 — Bibliothèque Brûlée. On warm ivory laid paper, crossed by a drifting casement of pale window-light, an enormous oxblood italic title runs behind one exquisitely drawn flask; the word seen through the glass is bent by it, and the amber-oxblood liquid inside tilts, sloshes, ripples and bubbles as the pointer passes or the flask is tipped. Scrolling carries the same flask into the olfactory pyramid, where its oak cap lifts off and the notes rise from the neck as fine wisps and then evaporate in order, top first, heart next, base lingering, before three quieter movements: a portrait of the perfumer beside a scorched, tea-ringed folio, the object's specifications with a dimensioned elevation and a discreet table of editions, and a one-line request for a sample card that is answered by an envelope closed with a wax seal. The voice is slow, ceremonial, French.
